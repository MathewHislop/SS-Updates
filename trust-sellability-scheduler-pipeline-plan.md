# The Full Pipeline: Trust → Sellability → Software → Scheduler

*This supersedes `trade-review-engine-scheduler-pipeline-plan.md` — same foundation (the review/marketing ladder, the peer-credibility hook, the trades-only narrowing), now built around the real positioning behind it.*

## The actual pitch

**Help aging, one-man-army tradies turn their business into something that survives them — and sells for more when they're ready to retire.**

This is a real, named category, not a story invented to sound good: it's the same principle behind John Warrillow's *Built to Sell* / Value Builder thinking — buyers and business brokers consistently pay a lower multiple for a business that's entirely dependent on the owner's head and phone number, and a meaningfully higher multiple for one with documented systems: a real customer database, a real job history, processes that don't collapse the day the owner stops showing up. That's not a vague pitch, it's a well-understood, concrete valuation lever, and it reframes everything already planned around it.

## Why this is stronger than the original framing

The original idea was "get trade businesses more Google reviews, use that as a trust-builder toward Scheduler." That's fine, but it competes on the same ground as every other reputation-management pitch. The sellability angle is different: it gives a **specific buyer** (an older tradie thinking about retirement, not just any trade business owner), a **specific pain point** (my business is worth less because it's all in my head), and a reason the whole ladder — reviews, marketing, and now real software — adds up to one coherent story instead of a bundle of separate services.

## The funnel, rebuilt around this positioning

1. **Trust-building entry point (unchanged from before):** the Review Engine — GBP setup, automated review requests, a one-time fee instead of a subscription, sold peer-to-peer ("I run a 25-person roofing crew, I built this for my own business first"). Still the cheapest, fastest way in the door. See the original plan for the full mechanics — nothing about this layer changes.
2. **The sellability conversation:** once there's trust, the actual pitch shifts from "let's get you more reviews" to "let's make your business look like something a buyer would actually want to pay a premium for." This is where the tone changes from marketing service to something closer to a business advisor relationship — a meaningfully bigger, higher-trust ask, which is exactly why it needs the Review Engine step first rather than leading with it cold.
3. **The software (new, concrete deliverable):** the scheduler/CRM tool already built is the tangible proof point of that pitch — a real customer database and job history, replacing "it's all in my head" or a paper diary. This is now doing double duty:
   - **Sold as a standalone downloadable product** to trade businesses generally (the plan already worked out — Lemon Squeezy, $99 intro/$189 standard, FB ads, landing page) — a real, separate revenue line reaching tradies who never become a Review Engine client at all.
   - **Given or bundled** to Mathew's own Review Engine/sellability clients as part of that service — the literal artifact that makes their business look "sale-ready," not just a nice add-on.
4. **The endgame:** once a client trusts you enough to have handed over their reviews, their customer data, and bought into the "get sale-ready" story, Scheduler is the natural full version of that system once it's live — the warmest possible lead pool for Scheduler's first paying customers, unchanged from the original plan.

## Two audiences for the same software, worth keeping distinct

- **External buyers** (via the downloadable product, FB ads, landing page): any small trade business wanting to own their scheduling/CRM outright instead of paying a subscription. Doesn't require the sellability pitch at all — "no monthly bill" is enough of a hook on its own.
- **Mathew's own service clients**: specifically framed around exit-readiness — older tradies who are further along the "thinking about retirement" path. This is a smaller, higher-touch, higher-trust group reached through the Review Engine funnel and referrals, not cold FB traffic.

Worth keeping the marketing language separate for each: subscription-fatigue messaging for the general downloadable-product audience, sellability/succession messaging for the service-client audience. Same product, two different reasons to want it.

## Where first customers come from (both tracks)

- **Software product audience:** FB ads (validated as the right platform of the three considered — it meaningfully over-indexes on older users versus Instagram/TikTok, a real fit for an older-tradie-leaning buyer), a Shopify or simple landing page as the front door, Lemon Squeezy handling checkout/licensing/delivery underneath it.
- **Sellability service audience:** the existing Review Engine channels — non-competing trades already crossed paths with, a Google Maps list filtered by low review counts, trade Facebook groups/associations, referrals — with an added, sharper qualifier worth screening for: owners visibly closer to retirement age, or ones who mention wanting to eventually sell or hand the business down. That's a much better-qualified lead than "any trade business with few reviews."

## Everything else from the original plan still holds

The hook ("I run a 25-person roofing crew myself..."), the trades-only narrowing, the one-time-fee-instead-of-subscription wedge against NiceJob/Birdeye, the phased side-hustle-to-full-time roadmap, and the eventual fold-in to Scheduler are all still the right shape — this update layers a sharper, more specific reason for a client to say yes on top of that structure, it doesn't replace it.
