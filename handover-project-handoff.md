# Project Handoff: [working name] — Trade Business Sellability

*Suggested name: **Handover** (or **Handover Ready**) — short, plain-English, and it says exactly what the business does: getting a trade business ready to hand over to a buyer. Rename freely; used as a placeholder below.*

## What this is

A service and software business helping aging, one-man-army tradies turn their business into something that survives them — and sells for a real premium when they retire. It's built on a well-established principle in small business sales: buyers and brokers pay less for a business that's entirely dependent on the current owner's head and phone number, and meaningfully more for one with documented systems — a real customer database, a real job history, processes that don't collapse when the owner steps back. This project sells the path from the first to the second.

It runs as three connected layers, not three separate products:
1. **Trust-building entry point** — a low-cost Review Engine (Google Business Profile setup + automated review requests, one-time fee instead of a subscription), sold peer-to-peer by someone who actually runs a trade business, not a generic marketing agency.
2. **The sellability service** — once trust is established, the real conversation: making the business look, function, and record-keep like something a buyer would pay a premium for.
3. **The software** — a real scheduling/CRM system, proof-in-hand of exactly that pitch, sold two ways: bundled into the sellability service for Handover's own clients, and separately as a standalone downloadable product to any small trade business that wants to own its tools outright instead of renting them monthly.

## Goals

1. **Validate the sellability pitch with real conversations** before assuming it's the right frame — talk to actual tradies who are within a few years of wanting to retire or sell, and confirm "make my business worth more when I sell it" lands harder than the original "get more reviews" pitch before building the service out further.
2. **Get the standalone software selling itself**, independent of the service business — a real revenue line reaching tradies who never become a Handover client at all, validating the "no subscription, own it outright" positioning against a market that's almost entirely monthly SaaS.
3. **Land a small number of real sellability clients** through the existing Review Engine funnel, using the software as the visible, tangible first deliverable of that relationship.
4. **Keep the two audiences and two pitches distinct** — subscription-fatigue messaging for the general software buyer, sellability/succession messaging for service clients — without letting the business become confused about which pitch it's making to whom.
5. **Build toward Scheduler adoption** as the natural endgame once it's live — Handover's own client base becomes its warmest first pool of paying customers.

## Outcomes

- **Short-term:** 3–5 real conversations with tradies close to retirement age confirming (or disproving) that the sellability framing is a stronger hook than generic marketing help; first real sales of the standalone downloadable software product through the FB ads → landing page → Lemon Squeezy funnel already planned.
- **Medium-term:** A handful of founding Review Engine clients converted into the fuller sellability conversation, with the software genuinely in use as their customer/job record system; enough standalone software sales to know whether the $99 intro / $189 standard pricing and positioning actually convert at real volume, not just in theory.
- **Longer-term:** A small, real base of sellability service clients paying for the fuller offering, a standalone software product generating its own independent revenue line, and — once Scheduler is live — a warm, pre-trusted pool of leads ready to move onto it.

## What this project is explicitly not

- Not a generic marketing agency — every piece of it (reviews, software, the sellability pitch itself) is built specifically around trade businesses, not "any small business."
- Not dependent on the software service being the whole business — it's one deliverable inside a bigger trust-and-value story, not a standalone SaaS play (that's Scheduler's job, later).
- Not competing with the downloadable software's own general audience — the sellability service and the standalone product sale are two different sales motions to two different (sometimes overlapping) audiences, and should stay messaged separately rather than merged into one pitch.
